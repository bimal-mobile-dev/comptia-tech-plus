## Computing basics

- **Input, processing, output, and storage**: the fundamental cycle every computing device follows — a keyboard providing input, a CPU processing it, a monitor providing output, and a drive storing the result
- Understanding this cycle at a conceptual level is the foundation for recognizing what role a given piece of hardware plays, tested more deeply in Domain 2 (Infrastructure)

## Notational systems and units of measure

- **Number systems**: binary, hexadecimal, decimal, and octal — computers fundamentally operate in binary, while hexadecimal is commonly used as a more human-readable shorthand (e.g., in color codes or memory addresses)
- **Units of measure**: storage (bit, byte, KB, MB, GB, TB), speed (MHz, GHz), and throughput (bps, Mbps, Gbps) — knowing which unit measures which kind of quantity (storage capacity vs. processing speed vs. network throughput) is a frequently tested distinction

## Troubleshooting methodology

- A structured approach: **identify the problem → establish a theory of probable cause → test the theory → establish a plan of action and implement the solution → verify full functionality → document findings** — this general methodology reappears (in fuller form) at the A+ level, making it worth learning correctly here
