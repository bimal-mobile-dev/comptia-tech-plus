## Confidentiality, integrity, and availability

- The **CIA triad** introduced at its simplest: keeping information private (confidentiality), accurate and unaltered (integrity), and accessible when needed (availability) — the same foundational concept that reappears, far more deeply, in every security certification in this collection (see Security+ Domain 1.2)

## Authentication and access control basics

- **Usernames, passwords, and basic MFA awareness**: recognizing why strong, unique passwords matter and what multi-factor authentication adds beyond a password alone
- **Basic access control concept**: understanding that not everyone should have access to everything — an introductory version of least privilege

## Common threats and safe practices

- **Recognizing common threats**: phishing, malware, and social engineering at an awareness level — spotting a suspicious email or link, not analyzing malware behavior
- **Basic safe computing practices**: keeping software updated, using antivirus protection, and avoiding untrusted downloads or links — practical, everyday security hygiene that forms the foundation Security+ and every advanced security certification builds further upon
